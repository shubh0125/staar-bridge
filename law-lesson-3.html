The Three Documents You Need Before You Can Do Almost Anything

Whether you want to open a bank account, get a job, sign a lease, enroll in school, get a library card, almost every "adult" thing you're about to do starts with the same question: prove who you are.

That comes down to three documents: your birth certificate, your Social Security card, and a state ID. Here's the thing nobody tells you — they don't work in isolation. You usually need one to get the next one. Get the order wrong and you can end up stuck in a loop, unable to get document B because you don't have document A yet. Here's how they connect, and exactly how to get each one in Ohio.

Why order matters

Your birth certificate is the foundation — it's the one document that proves your legal name, date of birth, and that you were born in the U.S., all in one place. Once you have it, it unlocks the other two:

  • Your birth certificate → gets you a Social Security card (if you don't already have one, or need to replace it).
  • Your birth certificate → gets you an Ohio state ID (paired with proof of your Social Security number and your address).
  • Your Social Security card + state ID → get you a bank account, a job, and a lease — the things you actually came here for.

If you only remember one thing from this article, make it this: get your birth certificate first. Everything else is faster once you have it.

The chain: birth certificate → unlocks your Social Security card and Ohio state ID → those two unlock a bank account, a job, and a lease.

1. Getting your birth certificate

If you were born in Ohio, Columbus Public Health's Office of Vital Statistics issues certified copies for births from 1908 to today. A few real details:

  • Cost: $25 per certificate.
  • Three ways to get one: by mail, online through VitalChek, or in person (same-day service is available walking in).
  • Mailing address: Office of Vital Statistics, Columbus Public Health, 240 Parsons Ave., Columbus, OH 43215. Phone: (614) 645-7331.
  • In person is usually fastest if you need it same-day — no appointment required.

If you weren't born in Ohio, you'll need to contact the vital records office in the state where you were born instead — every state runs its own.

2. Getting or replacing your Social Security card

Almost everyone already has a Social Security number — you just might not have the physical card, especially if it's been lost, or you've moved around a lot. Two different situations, two different processes:

If you've never had a Social Security number at all: you have to apply in person using Form SS-5, and bring your birth certificate plus one form of ID (like a school record or state ID if you have one). This one can't be done online, no matter your age.

If you already have a number but lost the card: it's easier. If you're 18 or older, have a U.S. mailing address, don't need a name change, and already have a driver's license or state ID from a participating state, you can request a replacement online through a "my Social Security" account at ssa.gov. No participating ID yet? You can still mail in Form SS-5 with one piece of ID, or bring it to a local Social Security office in person. Replacement cards are always free.

3. Getting your Ohio state ID

Here's genuinely good news: if you're 17 or older and don't already have a driver's license, Ohio gives you your first state ID card for free.

To get one, bring proof of:

  • Full legal name, date of birth, and legal presence in the U.S. — your birth certificate or a passport covers this.
  • Your Social Security number — your Social Security card or a W-2 covers this.
  • Your Ohio address — a pay stub, a signed lease, a school transcript, or a utility bill can all work (check bmv.ohio.gov's acceptable documents list, since handwritten items generally aren't accepted).

You can go to any Ohio BMV Deputy Registrar location — no appointment needed, though you can reserve a spot in line online if you'd rather not wait.

Before you start, do this

Make a photocopy or a phone photo of every document the moment you get it — front and back. If an original ever gets lost, having a copy makes replacing it dramatically faster, since you'll already know exactly what it said. Keep the copies somewhere separate from the originals — a folder at a trusted person's place, a cloud photo album, anywhere that isn't the same bag as the real thing.

Where to get real help

  • Columbus Public Health, Office of Vital Statistics — 240 Parsons Ave., Columbus, OH 43215, (614) 645-7331 — for an Ohio birth certificate.
  • Social Security Administration — ssa.gov — for a Social Security card, first-time or replacement.
  • Ohio BMV — bmv.ohio.gov — for a free Ohio state ID, plus the exact list of accepted documents.

None of these documents are complicated to get once you know the order. The hard part is usually just knowing where to start — now you do.

This article is for general educational purposes and isn't a substitute for legal advice. Requirements, fees, and processes can change — always verify current details with Columbus Public Health, the SSA, or the Ohio BMV before relying on them.
