Avoiding Predatory Loans & Scams: How to Spot the Trap Before You're In It

Here's the number that matters most in this entire post: borrowing $300 can cost you anywhere between $84 and $680 in fees and interest, depending entirely on where you borrow it from. Same amount of money, same borrower, potentially a $600 difference, just based on which door you walked through. That gap is the whole reason this lesson exists.

The Real Cost, Side by Side

Here's what borrowing $300 actually costs across four real options, assuming a roughly 3-month repayment period:

Where You BorrowTotal RepaidFees & InterestEffective Cost
Unlicensed/predatory lender (pre-reform era rates)~$980~$680Over 500% APR
Licensed Ohio short-term lender (legal cap)~$384~$8428% APR (legal max)
Credit union Payday Alternative Loan (PAL)~$384~$84~28% APR, but reported to build your credit
Rent-to-own on a $300 item~$750–$900~$450–$600Equivalent to 150–200%+ APR, dressed up as "monthly payments"

The pattern to notice: the monthly payment can look almost identical across all four options, while the total cost is wildly different. This is exactly how predatory lending survives: nobody markets the APR; everyone markets the "just $45 a month."

Ohio capped legal lenders at 28% APR in 2018, but that cap only applies to lenders actually licensed here. Online and "tribal" lenders that skip licensing can and do charge the old, uncapped rates, meaning the worst column in that table is still very much available to anyone who doesn't check first.

Do the Math Before You Sign Anything

Three numbers to always ask for, every time, before agreeing to borrow anything:

  1. The APR: not the monthly payment, the actual annualized rate. If a lender won't give you a straight answer, that's your answer.
  2. The total repayment amount: multiply the payment by the number of payments. Compare that number to what you're actually borrowing.
  3. The cost as a percentage of the loan: Ohio law caps this at 60% of principal for licensed short-term loans. If fees and interest combined exceed that, on a legal Ohio loan, something's wrong.

Where the Math Gets Hidden

Rent-to-own is the cleanest example of hidden math: a $300 item at "$35/month for 24 months" sounds manageable, until you multiply it out to $840, nearly triple the retail price, for something you could've bought outright with three or four months of payments.

Stacked buy-now-pay-later apps hide the math differently: no single payment looks dangerous, but four apps at $40 each due across the same week is $160 due at once, and each one carries its own separate late fee if you miscalculate.

Car title loans hide the real cost behind the collateral; the APR math works the same as a payday loan, but the actual risk if you can't pay isn't just fees; it's your car.

Real Alternatives, With Real Numbers

  • A credit union Payday Alternative Loan (PAL) is capped around the same 28% legal rate, but through an institution that reports your payments to build credit instead of just profiting off default. Ask Telhio or Wright-Patt directly.
  • NFCC credit counseling (nfcc.org) free and can often negotiate existing debt down rather than adding new debt on top of it
  • An emergency fund: the $500–$1,000 starting goal from Day 2 of this series exists specifically so this entire comparison table never has to matter to you in a real emergency

Get Started This Week

  • Link: com.ohio.gov: Ohio Department of Commerce licensed lender lookup, to check before you borrow from anyone
  • Link: nfcc.org: free, legitimate credit counseling if you're already carrying high-cost debt
  • Ask Telhio or Wright-Patt directly about Payday Alternative Loan (PAL) options before considering any outside lender
  • Do the "multiply it out" math from this post on any loan or rent-to-own offer before signing; a calculator and five minutes is the whole tool you need
